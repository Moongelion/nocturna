I ART ARCHIVE

The Kiss

Gustav Klimt

1907–1908 · Oil and gold leaf on canvas

DESCEND
I Context

Klimt's Vienna

The Kiss, painted between 1907 and 1908, belongs to Gustav Klimt's so-called Golden Period and represents one of the defining moments of his artistic language.

The work emerged in early twentieth-century Vienna, a city undergoing profound cultural transformation. Klimt was a founding member of the Vienna Secession, a movement that sought to move beyond academic conventions and explore new forms of artistic expression.

Rather than representing love through a specific narrative scene, Klimt transforms it into an almost timeless image. The two figures appear isolated against a golden background that removes almost every reference to a physical setting.

II Composition

The Language of Forms

The composition is organised around two intertwined figures that form a single visual unit. The male figure bends over the woman while both are enveloped by a golden surface that functions simultaneously as clothing, ornament and space.

Klimt establishes a deliberate contrast between the patterns covering the two figures. Rectangular, predominantly dark forms define the male figure, while circles, spirals and floral motifs appear throughout the woman's.

This opposition makes decoration an essential part of the composition. The bodies cease to exist independently from their ornamentation and gradually become inseparable from it.

III Symbolism

Gold, Body and Ornament

The use of gold transforms the scene into something that seems to exist beyond ordinary reality. The couple is not situated within a recognisable room or landscape. They exist within an abstract space constructed through light, colour and ornamentation.

The flowers surrounding the woman introduce a natural and delicate dimension in contrast with the geometry associated with the male figure. The difference between their patterns does not separate them; instead, it makes visible the way their bodies complement one another.

The embrace and the kiss form the centre of the work, yet Klimt avoids turning them into an explicit representation. Intimacy is contained within an ornamental structure that both protects and transforms the figures.

IV Interpretation

An Intimacy Suspended

One of the most powerful aspects of The Kiss is the absence of a before or an after. We do not know what happened before the encounter or what will happen afterwards. Klimt removes the narrative and preserves only the moment of closeness.

The couple seems to exist outside of time. The golden background does not function as a setting, but rather as a kind of absolute space in which the distinction between body, clothing and decoration begins to disappear.

The work can therefore be understood less as a specific scene and more as a representation of desire, intimacy and union. The kiss requires no story: the image transforms the gesture itself into the event.
